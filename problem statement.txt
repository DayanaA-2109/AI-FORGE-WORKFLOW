Smallholder tomato and vegetable farmers in Bihar lose 24–35% of their produce after harvest and are forced into distress sales, because heat accelerates spoilage within days and farm-gate cold storage is out of reach. Grid-tied cold stores are capital-heavy and fixed in location, so most smallholders cannot use them. No tool tells a farmer whether—given today’s heat and today’s mandi prices—to sell here, move to a better market, or hold.

This matters because Bihar scores 24/100 on NITI Aayog’s SDG 2 Index (2023–24)—the lowest among all Indian states—and India loses ₹31,486 crore annually on fruits and vegetables after harvest (ICAR-CIPHET, 2015), with fruit and vegetable losses of 4.58–15.88% nationally.
